How it works, and what it cannot see
  • The outer edge of each tire is traced along several hundred rays. Two equal circles in one plane pin down the camera: the squash of each gives the angle, the size difference gives the perspective. The photo is re-projected square-on and leveled.
  • The photo grade uses the camera angle, how much of each tire was traced, whether the front tire is out of round compared with the rear after correction (a turned wheel), and the resolution. The thresholds are provisional until phone photos have been tested.
  • Scale: every measurement you enter gives its own mm per pixel. They are combined, weighted by their tolerances, and each is shown against what the photo reads at the combined scale.
  • Anything that sits out from the frame centerline, such as the chainring, crank bolt or axle end caps, appears shifted when the camera is not square. The tool corrects the bottom bracket for the camera angle, assuming the outer chainring sits about 48 mm out (the crank bolt about 70 mm on the non-drive side), and takes the axles from the tire outlines instead of the end caps.
  • The seat tube angle is traced from the two edges of the seatpost and seat tube. The saddle top is where that line leaves the saddle's outline against the background.
  • The ± on each number comes from 200 random re-runs that jiggle the clicks, the tire fit, the camera solution and the scale. It does not include an error in the seat tube angle or in the chainring depth.
  • Not modeled: lens distortion, tires of different size front and rear, light-colored tires, or a saddle the same color as the wall behind it. Non-drive-side photos are supported by hand placement only and have not been tested.
  • Points and settings are remembered in this browser per photo.
